How I build teams

This is the short version of my talk on building teams that actually deliver.
How we work changes over time, the core principles do not.

Invert the pyramid

The team makes the calls. Leadership makes the goal clear and sorts out whatever’s stopping them. If every decision needs my approval, I’m the bottleneck.

Give people challenging work and room to grow.

McGregor on Theory Y · Maslow on motivation · Sutherland & Jordaan · Muñoz Pérez & Irún Molina

Context is critical

A ticket can be a keyhole view of the work. Everyone sees a small part and fills in the rest themselves.

Show the whole picture. Who’s this for, what are we trying to change, and what’s getting in the way?

Drucker on shared goals · Scrum Guide on transparency · Dale on learning through experience

Shared understanding of scope

We don’t have scope until we have a shared understanding of scope.

Problem:
What are we solving?
Boundaries:
What’s included and excluded?
Done:
What has to be true before we call it finished?

Agree on all three with the people building it. Take turns, ask before telling, and give people time to think.

Scrum Guide on planning & Definition of Done · Woolley and colleagues · Seagal & Horne

Understand cognitive bias

Bias shapes which evidence we notice and what we dismiss. Past experience, money already spent and who’s in the room can all influence the call.

Ask what would change our minds. Would we make the same call if we were starting today?

50 cognitive biases

Estimates are estimates

The people doing the work own the sizing, that’s it. Before arguing about hours or points, ask who needs the number and what decision it helps them make.

We’re all too good at imagining everything going to plan. Look at what similar work actually took, split up the big stuff, and leave room for what we don’t know.

Buehler, Griffin & Ross on the planning fallacy · Scrum Guide on sizing

Inspect and adapt

Pick a goal, deliver something people can use, then look at what happened. Keep releases small so customers get value sooner and we can change direction quickly.

The team runs the process, including the meetings. If we keep finding the same problem, we probably haven’t changed anything.

The original agile principles · Scrum Guide on reviews & retros

Summary

  • The team owns its meetings and process.
  • One vision, a clear purpose and shared goals.
  • One conversation, with everyone involved.
  • Share context and keep decisions visible.
  • Build a shared understanding of scope.
  • Keep tasks small enough to finish and get feedback quickly.
  • Have discussions when they’re needed, with just enough documentation.

My sources

Here's where I got my ideas from. No single person has all the answers, including me.

  • 50 Cognitive Biases in the Modern World Marcus Lu · Visual Capitalist · 2020 · Graphic by TitleMax

    50 ways my brain can confidently get it wrong. I sometimes tell people the most important “course” a PM could take is a session with a therapist.

  • The Human Side of Enterprise Douglas McGregor · PDF

    Trust people to do good work, then give them what they need.

  • A Theory of Human Motivation Abraham Maslow · 1943

    Start with the basics: do people feel safe, respected and part of the team?

  • Factors Affecting the Retention of Knowledge Workers Margie Sutherland & Wilhelm Jordaan · 2004

    306 people surveyed. Interesting work and room to grow matter.

  • Management by Objectives and Self-Control Peter F. Drucker · Chapter excerpt

    If nobody can explain why we’re doing the work, we’ve skipped a fairly important step.

  • The Scrum Guide Ken Schwaber & Jeff Sutherland · 2020

    Agree on the work, get it done, work out what to change.

  • Exploring the “Planning Fallacy”: Why People Underestimate Their Task Completion Times Roger Buehler, Dale Griffin & Michael Ross · 1994 · PDF · Additional study

    Start with how long it took last time. Apparently “this time will be different” isn’t much of an estimation method.

  • Principles behind the Agile Manifesto Agile Manifesto authors · 2001

    Ship something useful, talk to people, adjust. The manifesto is a pretty short read.

  • Human Dynamics for the 21st Century Sandra Seagal & David Horne · 2003 · PDF

    Some people think out loud. Others need a minute. Leave room for both.

  • Audio-Visual Methods in Teaching Edgar Dale · Cone of Experience · Book excerpt · PDF

    Draw it, demo it, or let people try it. Repeating it louder has limits.

  • Leadership and Teamwork in Innovation Ecosystems José Miguel Muñoz Pérez & Manuel Irún Molina · 2017

    People need to be comfortable disagreeing with the boss. Otherwise it’s a very polite echo chamber.

  • Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance of Human Groups Anita Williams Woolley and colleagues · 2010 · Additional study

    Teams that shared the airtime tended to do better. A reminder for me to stop talking occasionally.
